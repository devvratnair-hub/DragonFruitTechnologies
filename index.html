How I think

Architecture is the set of decisions that keep a system true when real life happens.

Six questions I bring to every Dayforce design, and where I have used them. Clients stay unnamed; the thinking doesn't.

01

Start from Monday morning

Who runs this after go-live, and what will they need to run it with confidence?

In practice · ADP Workforce Now

I configured Core HR, Benefits and Talent, then trained the administrators who would own them. Every design choice met the people who would live with it.

02

Find the source of truth

Which system owns this record, and at which moment?

In practice · Public-sector Dayforce

Candidates and employees lived in two separate Dayforce environments. I specified how their records link and the import rationale that keeps both in step.

03

Model the rules before the screens

What does the policy actually say, including its exceptions?

In practice · Public-sector Dayforce

Priority entitlements decide who must be considered first. I configured those rules in Recruiting so entitled candidates are handled correctly from requisition onward.

04

Map authority, then build the workflow

Who holds the authority to approve, and at which level?

In practice · Public-sector Dayforce

I designed and built approval workflows that route every Letter of Offer to the correct delegated approver, so approvals follow the delegation of authority exactly.

05

Design for the exception

What happens beyond the happy path?

In practice · Public-sector Dayforce

Acting appointments are temporary by nature. I designed the data and the end-to-end processing so they start, run and end cleanly, a pattern standard configurations rarely anticipate.

06

Separate confirmed from assumed

What have we verified, and what still needs to be confirmed?

In practice · Public-sector Dayforce

I staged and validated conversion data before loads and triaged defects to their real root cause: configuration, data or design. Readiness decisions rested on evidence.

Meet the founder

My journey began in Mumbai, in the BPO industry. That is where I discovered where my real passion lay: in people, and in how organizations find and grow them.

I moved into Talent Acquisition and Talent Development and spent several years doing that work. Wanting a wider view of the world, I went to the UK for my MBA in Human Resource Management, then joined a healthcare recruiting agency in London as HR Manager, where I learned the legal and compliance side of HR first-hand.

Canada came unexpectedly. At the University of Winnipeg I completed a post-graduate HR program that deepened my grounding in Talent Acquisition, Talent Management and Talent Development. Then I was given the opportunity to join Ceridian, now Dayforce, in 2017.

Moving from functional to technical work was the biggest stretch of my career, and I embraced it fully. Every role since, at ADP, then with global consulting teams on a large public-sector Dayforce transformation, taught me what technology consulting really is, and where to keep growing.

Those years taught me what leadership is. It isn't a designation. It is owning your work, showing up when it matters most, voicing risks early, championing change when it's needed, and standing by your values as a professional and as a human being.

Life has taught me through its highs and lows, professionally and personally, and those lessons inspired me to start Dragonfruit Technologies. I want to build a culture of collaboration, where people are free to speak, are backed and believed in, and are given respect where it is due, and where people's potential is nurtured and celebrated.

I believe change starts with one person. I intend to be that person, and to help the teams I work with do the same.
